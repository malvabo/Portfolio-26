Closing the compliance loop between co‑ops and importers

Reframed a parsing brief into a decision-and-follow-up product, and shipped it in eight weeks across five importer teams and their African suppliers.

The problem

Most documents that arrive are not usable

A coffee and cocoa trader wanted to automate the compliance paperwork moving between African supplier co‑ops and European importers. Certificates arrived expired. Issuers were absent from the registry. Photos were too dark to read, or several documents came bundled into one file. A rejected declaration stalls a container and ties up working capital.

The brief I inherited was to parse data out of those documents and show it to the importer. That was technically reasonable and would not have solved anything. If the certificate underneath is expired, a perfectly extracted expiry date only tells the officer faster that they now have to get a new one from a co‑op who does not use email.

Two ends, two worlds

What each side needed

The co‑op, at origin

Shared phones, patchy signal, WhatsApp as the default channel. Nobody watching an inbox. Needs one instruction and one large target.

The importer, in Europe

Hundreds of shipments audited one document at a time across email and ad hoc tools. Needs a verdict at a glance, not a filled form.

Nothing sat between them, so every correction was somebody manually chasing somebody else. I ran the supplier research through the in‑country consultants who meet these co‑ops in person, keeping the questions concrete: what device, what signal, who takes the photo, what happens the day after a rejection.

Approach

What I changed

  • 1.Made the output a decision. Every document resolves to validated, needs review, or send back. Extraction became a step inside that rather than the product.
  • 2.Closed the loop on WhatsApp. A failed document triggers an alert to the supplier with a link straight into a mobile re‑upload, so the importer stops being the courier.
  • 3.Handed the schema to the importer. Upload a sample, we propose the fields, they keep what matters.
  • 4.Deferred the learning loop. The right long‑term move, and it needed confirmed data the product did not have yet.

Strategic moment

Giving away the definition of “correct”

V1 defined the extraction schema for you, from document types we had already seen. Scale broke it, since requirements shift by country and by importer–supplier pair. Rather than keep modelling every variant, I moved the schema into the importer’s hands. It made the product sellable past the first few customers, and it put the definition of a valid document with the party accountable for it.

Craft

A visual language built from scratch

No design system to inherit, so the interface language had to be defined before it could be applied. Nearly every screen is a list of things in a state, so I built status as the load‑bearing primitive: it had to survive being the only signal on a row and extend to states we had not invented yet.

Accessibility set the constraints rather than following them. The hardest user is on a shared low-end phone, on an intermittent connection, reading a second language. Status never depends on colour alone, the supplier page carries its meaning in text before images load, and the officer side is reachable by keyboard at volume.

Impact

What shipped

Officers moved from opening every attachment to handling only what the system flags, and the chasing they used to do by hand now goes out on its own. Five importer teams onboarded along with their suppliers.

On measurement, I will be straight: this launched without the instrumentation to claim a number on error rates, so I am not going to invent one. The metric worth moving is bad documents caught before submission.

Closing insight

The most useful thing I did was refuse the scope

Parsing was a real request from a paying client, and it solved the visible half. Showing the field evidence that the cost sat in the other half is what changed the outcome. I could not observe my hardest users directly, so I designed around what I could establish with certainty about their conditions rather than a persona I had imagined.
