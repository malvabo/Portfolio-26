Conduit

Turning a fixed compliance engine into pipelines importers build themselves

Shipped an MVP with our compliance rules baked in, then let testing reframe the product around rules each customer sets.

Role

Product and design lead

Scope

Product direction, design, design system

Timeframe

Four weeks

Domain

Supply chain

Impact summary

  • •Moved the product from our compliance rules to pipelines importers configure
  • •Made the pipeline the unit: built once, saved, reused, called over an API
  • •Split the work into Extract and Inject, each useful on its own
  • •Cut the validation layer to keep the second version shippable

The problem & context

Conduit automates the paperwork that clears food and produce shipments through customs. The documents are high volume, repetitive, and sit on the critical path, so one wrong field holds a container at the port. I mapped how the operation actually ran before we designed anything.

  • 1.Compliance documents were high‑volume, repetitive and error‑prone
  • 2.They sat on the critical path for clearing shipments
  • 3.Nothing made the work reusable from one shipment to the next

User needs

In interviews, importers described the job as “the same paperwork every shipment, filled a little differently.” They needed the setup to carry over to the next shipment, and to follow their own reading of the rules, not ours.

Business goal

I was brought in to automate the documents on the critical path in a way a varied customer base could actually run, so the product could sell beyond a single interpretation of compliance.

The pipeline

The pipeline became the unit of the product: a recipe of fields to pull or fill, saved once and reused on every document you point it at.

The fork the product opens on: pull data out, or fill data in.

Extract: name the fields to pull, on a real supplier document.

Configure: name the fields to pull, each with a type and a description.

Inject: detect a form’s layout and fill it with the values you bring.

Pipelines are saved and reused. Runs keeps a plain record of what they have processed.

Approach

My strategy was to stop being the authority on compliance and become the tool importers use to build their own. To accomplish this, I…

  • 1.Mapped how importers actually work, and let the gaps set the first release.
  • 2.Made the pipeline the unit: one setup, built once and reused.
  • 3.Split it into two jobs that stand alone: Extract pulls data out, Inject fills forms in.
  • 4.Drafted the fields from a plain description, so setup was never a blank page.
  • 5.Saved every pipeline, so it could be duplicated or called from a customer’s own systems.

Describe the document and the schema drafts itself, ready to adjust.

Every saved pipeline gets an endpoint, so customers can run it from their own systems.

Strategic moment: from our rules to theirs

The MVP shipped with a built-in rules engine, our reading of what compliance required. Testing showed the flaw: compliance bends with the country and the product, so one fixed ruleset never fit a customer base this varied.

Dropping it was the hard call. The rules engine was the opinionated core the product had been sold on, and letting it go meant trusting customers to set their own rules instead of us.

That trade is what made Conduit sellable. Each importer now configures the pipeline to the rules they actually work under, rather than the ones we guessed at.

The design system underneath

There was no design system to inherit, so I built one: tokens, components, states and motion documented in one place. I animated the parsing stage myself, so a document being read has a state of its own rather than a dead spinner.

The reading‑the‑document animation, from the system’s Motion section.

States and rules documented in the system, not just built in the app.

Impact

Five teams ran the MVP inside their own workflow, and their testing is what reframed the product. A fixed engine sells one reading of the rules; a pipeline each customer configures fits their own, which is the difference between a demo and something a varied base can run.

Closing insight

Hard‑coding compliance was the fastest way to learn that compliance cannot be hard‑coded for a base this varied. The MVP earned its keep by being wrong in a useful way.
