Closing the document loop in cross‑border supply chain compliance

Every coffee and cocoa container that crosses a border carries a stack of compliance paperwork. Collecting it, checking it and correcting it is manual work, and it falls on the importer.

Role

Product and design lead

Scope

Research, product, design system

Timeframe

~8 weeks, kickoff to launch

Reach

5 importer teams and their suppliers

Importer workspace: shipment folders, document statuses, validation result

The problem

Most documents cannot be used as sent

Rural co‑operatives in Africa send compliance documents to European importers over WhatsApp, photographed on shared phones across patchy connections. At the other end a compliance officer opens each attachment in turn, checks it against the requirements for that trade lane, and files it. Almost none of the process is automated.

Most of what arrives is not usable. Certificates have expired. Issuers are absent from the registry. Photos are too dark or too skewed to read. Unrelated documents arrive bundled into one file. A rejected declaration stalls a container and ties up working capital.

The reframe

Extraction was only half the solution

The original brief was to parse data out of the documents and present it to the importer. Extraction was achievable, since the client had enough historical documents across enough suppliers to train against.

It did not close the loop. When the underlying certificate has expired, a correctly extracted expiry date only surfaces the failure faster. The importer still has to obtain a replacement from a supplier who does not use email, which is where the time actually went.

Thirty‑plus interviews on the supplier side, run through the in‑country consultants who meet these co‑operatives in person, established the constraints the follow‑up had to work within: shared devices, intermittent signal, WhatsApp as the only reliable channel.

Solution

Validate, then follow up automatically

The system validates rather than only extracting. It checks whether the certificate is in date, whether the issuer is recognised, and whether the file is legible enough to stand behind. Every value links back to its place in the source image, so a doubtful field takes one click to confirm.

Validated

In date, issuer recognised, read cleanly. The shipment moves.

Needs review

Expiring soon, or a field the system is unsure about.

Send back

Expired, unreadable, or failing a rule. Triggers the re‑request.

A send‑back fires the follow‑up on its own: a WhatsApp alert to the supplier and an email to the primary compliance contact with compliance managers copied, carrying a link into a mobile page built for one task, replacing that document. The importer sees the request and its state without leaving the shipment.

Scaling it

A schema each importer defines

The first version set the extraction schema itself, from document types already seen. Scale broke that. Requirements shift by country, and a given importer and supplier pair can need something different again.

The schema moved into the importer’s hands. They upload a sample document, the system proposes the fields worth pulling from it, and they keep what matters to their lane. This removed the need to model every new trade lane before selling into it, and it put the definition of a valid document with the party accountable for it.

Craft

A visual language built from scratch

There was no existing design system, so the interface language was defined before it was applied. Nearly every screen is a list of things in a state, which made status the load‑bearing primitive: it had to survive being the only signal on a row and extend to states not yet invented.

Accessibility set those constraints rather than following them. The hardest user is on a shared low-end phone, on an intermittent connection, reading a second language. Status never depends on colour alone, the supplier page carries its meaning in text before images load, and the officer side stays reachable by keyboard at volume.

Outcome

A complete workflow

Upload the document, the system extracts and validates it against the importer’s own schema, and either clears the shipment or requests a corrected copy from the supplier on the channel they already use. Officers work the exceptions their inbox flags instead of opening every attachment in order.
