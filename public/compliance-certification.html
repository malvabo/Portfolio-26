Automating compliance questionnaires for supply chain operations

A compliance platform for food supply chains. Two capabilities: answering counterparty questionnaires from a company’s own evidence, and validating that evidence before it leaves the building.

Role

Product and design lead

Scope

Research, product, design system

Timeframe

~8 weeks, kickoff to launch

Domain

Food supply chain

Questionnaire workspace: source questions, generated answers, supporting evidence

Use case one

Compliance questionnaires

Every company moving food across a supply chain faces the same request. When a counterparty evaluates a shipment or a supplier, they send a spreadsheet or a PDF with dozens of questions about the produce and the controls around it: origin and traceability, certification status, storage and handling, residue testing, the paperwork behind each claim.

Answering these manually is tedious. Teams dig through certification reports, internal policies and previously answered questionnaires to find each answer, then copy and paste responses into whatever format arrived this time.

The product automates it. A customer uploads the questionnaire alongside their supporting documents. The system parses the contextual evidence, extracts every question that needs an answer, and generates a response for each one grounded in the customer’s own documentation.

The reframe

Generating answers was only half the solution

Without a way to produce the final deliverable, customers still faced the copy and paste work the product was supposed to remove. A list of correct answers is not what the counterparty asked for. They asked for their document, completed.

So the flow had to close: AI‑generated answers written back into the original file with its exact formatting preserved. Upload the questionnaire, review the generated answers, download a completed document ready to send.

Use case two

Evidence evaluation, the layer behind the platform

The second capability validates every piece of compliance evidence uploaded. It checks captured screenshots for completeness, verifies formatting, and confirms nothing is missing before the evidence reaches a counterparty or an auditor.

It is not only a customer‑facing feature. A web agent captures compliance evidence and sends it to the evaluator, which identifies the gaps and feeds them back so the agent can iterate until the evidence is complete. The same system checks policy drafts, confirming that required sections are present and aligned to the relevant standard. It gives the other agents in the platform a feedback loop.

Evidence evaluation: captured evidence with gaps identified

Craft

A visual language built from scratch

There was no existing design system, so the interface language was defined before it was applied. Almost every screen is a list of things in a state: questions answered or unanswered, evidence complete or incomplete. That made status the load‑bearing primitive, and it had to survive being the only signal on a row and extend to states not yet invented.

Accessibility set those constraints rather than following them. Status never depends on colour alone, each screen carries its meaning in text before images load, and review work stays reachable by keyboard at volume.

Outcome

A complete work artifact

The customer uploads a questionnaire, the system answers it from their own evidence, and they download the finished document in the format the counterparty expects. Behind that, every piece of evidence has been checked for completeness before it is used.
